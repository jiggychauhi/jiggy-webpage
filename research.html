Quantum Computing

Quantum Kernel SVM for Parkinson's Disease Classification

View description

We explored Parkinson's disease classification from gait video recordings by representing them with covariance descriptors on Riemannian manifolds and training support vector machines. Alongside the classical RBF kernel, we benchmarked several quantum kernels, including Z, ZZ, and custom covariance-based feature maps. By combining these quantum-enhanced kernels with common spatial pattern filtering, we highlight how quantum approaches can enrich machine learning pipelines for studying neurodegenerative diseases.

[Quantum Mach. Intell. 7, 64 (2025)]

Quantum Denoising for BCI

View description

We conducted research on passive Brain-Computer Interfaces using EEG data, focusing on self-supervised learning and the application of quantum autoencoders for signal denoising. Our study demonstrated the technical feasibility of quantum autoencoding with real EEG time-series data and opened new avenues for exploring quantum approaches in neuroscience and brain-computer interfaces.

Single-Exponential Algorithms for Euclidean Color-Spanning Problems

View description

We studied Euclidean color-spanning problems, where points carry one of k colors and a feasible solution must include at least one point of each color. The objectives range from closest-pair and Steiner-tree problems to convex hulls of minimum area or perimeter. We investigated their parameterized complexity and developed single-exponential fixed-parameter algorithms, with running times such as O*(2k) and O*(3k). These bounds isolate the exponential dependence in the number of colors, while the remaining dependence on input size is polynomial; the star suppresses those polynomial factors.

2D Materials

Twisted MoS2 Devices and Automated Stacking Workflows

View description

My visit to the Liaoning Academy of Materials was my first hands-on experience working with van der Waals materials. I learned mechanical exfoliation, optical identification of flakes, and dry-transfer stacking while building a twisted MoS2 device. Working through the fabrication process taught me how flake selection, alignment, and controlled pickup come together to create a layered heterostructure. I also explored computer-vision tools for tracking Newton rings and detecting flake pickup, as a first step toward automating the stacking workflow. I presented this work in my LAM talk, “Fabrication, Devices, and Automated Workflows in 2D Materials Research.”

Quantum Materials

Anisotropic Transport of Josephson Vortices in Atomic-Layer Superconductors

View description

We investigated superconductivity in atomic-layer indium films on silicon substrates, focusing on how surface steps influence Josephson vortex motion. Using an ultrahigh-vacuum STM and low-temperature transport setup, we studied the response to magnetic fields and compared transport parallel and perpendicular to the steps to explore the role of the vicinal surface in vortex dynamics.

[Phys. Rev. B 114, 055422 (2026)]

Growth and Characterization of BST Topological Insulator Thin Films

View description

We grew epitaxial bismuth antimony telluride, (BixSb1−x)2Te3 (BST), thin films on sapphire using molecular beam epitaxy. The project combined substrate preparation and growth optimization with AFM, RHEED, X-ray diffraction, and energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy to characterize film morphology, structure, and composition. We also prepared Hall-bar devices for low-temperature transport measurements, examining resistance and Hall response to assess bulk conduction and the challenges of accessing surface-state transport.
